\lettersection{Why Vietnamese, why Siri}
I am applying for the Siri Language Engineer (Vietnamese) role. I am a Vietnamese-native machine learning engineer, and for the past two years I have been building Vietnamese speech systems in production: zero-shot text-to-speech, voice cloning, and the data pipelines behind them. Today I adapt English TTS, vision-language and embedding models to Vietnamese for Viettel's Personal AI phone, built on Qualcomm's reference design and SDK. The question your team owns, whether a Vietnamese speaker feels understood by the device, is the question I have been working on every day.

\lettersection{What I bring}
I built SpeechPie, a 7-step pipeline that collected and curated 10,000 hours of Vietnamese speech, and SpeechCake, a training and inference pipeline for zero-shot TTS that we extended to voice design and paralinguistic generation across 14 Vietnamese tags. I designed the benchmarks and listening tests we used to compare systems, and the resulting model reached state-of-the-art voice cloning and speech editing results against Vietnamese TTS baselines. I have also contributed to eight publications, including EMNLP 2026 and INTERSPEECH 2026, and I work comfortably across data generation, model training and evaluation, which is the loop this role owns. My English is professional working proficiency (IELTS 8.0), and I am ready to relocate to Singapore.

\lettersection{Why Apple}
Language quality at Apple's scale is a different problem from a research benchmark: it has to hold up for millions of speakers, on-device, across dialects and code-switching, with privacy built in. That is exactly the constraint I find interesting, and Vietnamese is a language where small quality differences are very visible to users. I would like to bring my production experience in Vietnamese speech to the team that ships it to them.

\end{cvletter}
